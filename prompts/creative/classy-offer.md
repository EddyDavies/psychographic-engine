# Format: Classy Offer

> Two product variants side-by-side with elegant engagement question. Premium feel, not discount-bin.
> Adapted from: 10-classy-offer

## Variables

| Variable | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| `reference_images_left` | First product variant (cutout preferred) | `products/images/kitchen-spray/cutout.jpg` |
| `reference_images_right` | Second product variant (cutout preferred) | `products/images/glass-spray/cutout.jpg` |
| `headline` | Engagement question (under 20 chars) | "Left or right?" |
| `offer_text` | Elegant offer reveal (under 60 chars) | "Pick your favourite. We'll add a month of pods free." |

## Prompt

### [FORMAT]

1:1 square preferred. Clean, minimal, elegant.

### [BACKGROUND]

Light gray (#F5F5F5) or white. Clean, no texture, no environment. The simplicity IS the design.

### [PRODUCTS]

Two product variants from reference images, side by side, equal size, centred in the frame. Each in its own subtle framed area. Same photographic style, same angle, same lighting — they look like a deliberate pair. Products are the visual centrepiece.

### [COPY_OVERLAY]

Top — elegant serif headline, black, centred:
"{headline}"
Simple, engaging, creates interaction urge.

Bottom — serif body text, black, centred:
"{offer_text}"
Elegant, understated. The offer is revealed here, not shouted.

### [STYLE]

Premium, minimal, elegant. This is a "sales ad" that doesn't feel like a sales ad. The engagement question creates interaction. Both options are YOUR product — there's no wrong answer. Think high-end brand email, not Facebook clearance. Clean white space, serif typography, quiet confidence.

### [LEARNINGS]

- Both products MUST be the same brand — the "left or right?" only works when both choices benefit you
- Cutout product images work best — styled images add visual noise that competes with the minimal design
- Serif fonts render well in NanoBanana and feel more premium than sans-serif for this format
- Keep the offer text elegant — "Pick your favourite" beats "BUY ONE GET ONE FREE"
- 1:1 square works best — 9:16 creates too much empty space in this minimal design
- The simplicity is fragile — adding ANY extra elements (badges, pills, bullets) breaks the premium feel
