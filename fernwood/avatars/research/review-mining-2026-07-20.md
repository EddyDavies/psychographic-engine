## 3. Pains
- **Eco spray smears glass:** functional (streaky windows) and emotional (felt foolish for trying; "conned for being a good person")
- **Bottle clutter:** half-used bottles nobody finishes; in a flat share, nobody owns the problem
- **Subscription pile-up:** past subscriptions that delivered more than was used and were hard to cancel
- **Cupboard under the sink:** a newly mobile toddler; the parent cannot tell which bottles are worth worrying about
- **Weaning mess:** wiping the tray three times a day, and the smell of spray where the baby eats

## 4. Before/After States
- **Before:** "Windows done twice, still streaky, opened every bottle to sniff which was the good one." **After:** "One wipe on the bathroom mirror and I actually went and got my partner to look."
- **Before:** "Shelf full of mystery bottles with someone else's name on." **After:** "One tin on the windowsill and four bottles. Even my flatmates refill them."
- **Before:** "Everything harsh moved to the top of the fridge." **After:** "The tin lives on the high shelf. The bottles I made myself, so I know what's in them."

## 5. Emotional Triggers
- **Urgency events:** baby starting to crawl or open cupboards; moving flat; running out of spray on a cleaning day
- **Social pressure:** guests seeing smeared mirrors; flatmate arguments about who buys supplies
- **Aspirational moments:** a friend's tidy cleaning cupboard; seeing someone drop a pod in and spray straight away

## 6. Objections
- "Eco products never clean as well" (overcome by: the guarantee, a friend's recommendation, seeing the glass)
- "It's just going to be another subscription" (overcome by: the kit being a one-off; "I checked twice that nothing renews")
- "A pod is exactly the kind of thing a toddler puts in their mouth" (overcome by: the tin's push-and-twist lid and keeping it on a high shelf; "fewer things to hide, not more")
- "Glass bottles in a house with a toddler?" (overcome by: silicone sleeves, or using their own plastic bottle)
- "My flatmates will use it and never refill it" (overcome by: refilling taking ten seconds; "even they manage it")

## 7. Unexpected Uses
- All-purpose pod used to clean the inside of the car and the dog's bowl area
- Glass pod used on the oven door and the TV screen
- Parents keep one bottle permanently by the high chair "so it's always there"

## Top 10 Insights
1. The strongest pain is not plastic; it is having tried to go green and been let down by the clean.
2. Glass is the customer's own test. "The mirror is my test."
3. "Conned for being a good person": the let-down is personal, so proof beats promises.
4. Clutter is felt most where space is shared: flat shares and the cupboard under the sink.
5. Subscription fear is about pile-up and cancelling, not price.
6. Parents do not want "safe" claims; they want to know what is in the bottle and to have fewer things to hide.
7. Weaning parents wipe the same surface many times a day and notice the smell most there.
8. "It's just a tablet and water?" disbelief is a curiosity gap, not an objection.
9. Refilling has to be easy enough that the least bothered person in the house does it.
10. Customers describe the result in scenes (the mirror, the shelf, the fridge top), which is where hooks should come from.
