# Sub-Avatar: sub-subscription-sceptic

> Fictional worked example. Fernwood is not a real brand.

## Parent Core Avatar
[core-low-waste-household](../profile.md)

## Niche Gap
- **Situation:** Wants to switch to refills but has been burned by a subscription that kept delivering more than they used and was a pain to cancel, so they now check the small print of every product before buying.
- **Unique Objection:** "It's just going to be another subscription I can't get out of."
- **Different Value Prop:** The value is ownership and no commitment: buy the kit once, buy pods only when the tin is empty, and dry pods never go off in the drawer.
- **Three Questions:** different ad: yes (leads with "one-off, nothing renews"); unique objection: yes (subscription trap); situation alone: yes (burned by a subscription, reads the terms first)

> The niche gap test (see `../../README.md`). All three must be yes. A "no" means the gap is weak: name the sub-avatar it overlaps and merge rather than keep it.

## Layered Categories
> Minimum 2 Core 5 categories combined
1. **Desire:** Switch to refills without being locked into anything
2. **Experience:** A past subscription piled up and was hard to cancel
3. **Behaviour:** Checks cancellation terms and "does this renew?" before buying anything

## Full Description
> One sentence combining all categories:
"They want to stop buying plastic bottles, but a past subscription buried them in refills they never asked for, so they distrust anything that delivers on a schedule and read the small print before they buy."

## Implied Beliefs
> Decomposed from the belief "refill brands just want to lock you in":
- **Experience:** "Because a subscription kept sending refills they didn't need and made cancelling hard..."
- **Emotion:** "...they feel wary and a bit stupid for falling for it..."
- **Behavior:** "...so they look for the catch, avoid anything that says 'auto', and buy one-offs even when they cost more."

## Hooks That Resonate
1. "Buy it once. Nothing renews.": addresses the fear in the first line
2. "Pods don't go off.": removes the pile-up anxiety
3. "No plan. No drawer of refills.": their own scene, negated

> Note (2026-09-01): convenience hooks ("never reorder again") made this sub-avatar's fear worse. See `../../../testing/learnings.md`.

## Awareness Level
**Solution Aware**

## Objections
> Specific to this sub-avatar
- "It's just going to be another subscription I can't get out of": burned before
- "I'll end up with a drawer of refills I don't need": the pile-up was the real pain
- "What's the catch with the cheap kit price?": low entry price reads as bait for a plan
