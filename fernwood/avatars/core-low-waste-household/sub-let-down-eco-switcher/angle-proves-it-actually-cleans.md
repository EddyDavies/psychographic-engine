# Angle: Proves It Actually Cleans

> Fictional worked example. Fernwood is not a real brand.

**Reason to Buy:** The Starter Kit cleans glass streak-free from one pod and tap water, so going green no longer means a worse clean.

**Emotional Lever:** The let-down of her first switch, which she now blames on eco products in general. Profile: Implied Beliefs ("she feels let down, even conned for trying to do the right thing") and Behaviour ("tests every new cleaner on the bathroom mirror first"). The ad starts from her belief that eco products never clean as well, and proves the clean on her own test instead of arguing with it.

## Hooks

> Hooks communicate the angle. Keep them DIRECT (not indirect).
> Each hook should be usable as a headline on a static ad or opening line of a video.

1. **"Your eco spray smeared the mirror. This won't."**
   inspired_by: test result, c01 (won the Discovery batch); review mining, "my windows have never looked worse"

2. **"One pod. Tap water. Streak-free glass."**
   inspired_by: brainstorm from the mechanism (`products/glass-spray.md`)

3. **"Finally an eco spray that actually cleans."**
   inspired_by: review mining, golden phrase (customer's own words)

## Proof Points

> Only claims the product or offer file supports, each with where it comes from. Never fabricate volumes, names, or stories (see `engine/method/copywriting.md`).

- Streak-free on glass and mirrors when wiped with the dry microfibre cloth (source: `products/glass-spray.md`)
- The microfibre glass cloth comes in the kit (source: `offers/starter-kit.md`)
- Full-dose concentrate: every bottle is the same strength (source: `products/all-purpose-spray.md`, Assumed Unique Mechanisms)
- 30-day clean-or-refund guarantee (source: `offers/starter-kit.md`)
- Plant-based formula, no ammonia (source: `products/glass-spray.md`)

## Objections to Pre-empt

> Taken from the sub-avatar's Objections section. Body copy answers each with Objection, Claim, Proof, Benefit.

- "Eco products never clean as well": never argue; show one wipe on a mirror in the creative, then the guarantee as the safety net
- "It'll be fine on the worktop but useless on glass": lead with glass, the hardest test, so the worktop is assumed
- "I've already wasted money on one of these": the guarantee in the last line of body copy ("if it doesn't, we refund the kit")
