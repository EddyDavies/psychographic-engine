# Avatars: The Core 5 Framework

## What Is an Avatar

Not a demographic profile. A psychographic portrait. An avatar captures how someone thinks, feels, and behaves -- not just who they are on paper. The goal: create instant relatability ("that's exactly me"). When a customer sees your ad and feels deeply understood, you've nailed the avatar.

## The Core 5 Categories

Listed in order of importance for avatar building:

### 1. Desires

"I want" or "I need" statements. **Start here.** Desires are the engine of all action.

Map to the 6 Mass Instincts:

- **Health** -- Survival, stay alive, stay healthy, avoid disease
- **Sex** -- Be attractive, find a mate, keep a mate, be desirable
- **Status** -- Be important, respected, admired, recognised
- **Belonging** -- Fit in, have friends, be part of a group, be accepted
- **Control** -- Have power, predict outcomes, feel safe, reduce uncertainty
- **Comfort** -- Make life easier, avoid pain, increase pleasure, save time

Use surface-level desires for specificity (not "I want to be healthy" but "I want to wake up without back pain").

**Desire Power Rankings** (highest to lowest reach/urgency): Health > Status > Sex > Comfort > Control > Belonging. Higher-ranked instincts access larger audiences and trigger faster action. See `../concepts/desire-forces.md` for the full Desire Forces framework including elevation strategy, trending vs permanent desires, and desire channeling.

**Category Checker:** Can you say "I want X" or "I need X"? If not, it's not a desire.

### 2. Experiences

Situations (past or present) or product experiences (tried X, it didn't work). Isolate the event -- don't include emotions or desires in the description.

Two types:

- **Situational:** Life circumstances (just had a baby, starting a new job, going through a breakup)
- **Product-Based:** Solutions tried and their outcomes (tried competitor X but it broke after 2 months)

**Category Checker:** Does this describe a circumstance or event? Did we remove the emotion from it?

### 3. Emotions

How people feel. Understanding the emotional landscape is key to resonant copy.

- **6 Primary Emotions** (universal): Joy, Sadness, Fear, Anger, Surprise, Disgust
- **Secondary Emotions** (need unpacking): anxious, frustrated, motivated, insecure, overwhelmed, hopeful, guilty, proud...

Always trace secondary emotions back to their primary root. "Frustrated" -> Anger. "Anxious" -> Fear.

**Category Checker:** Does this describe how they feel ABOUT or IN RESPONSE to something?

### 4. Behaviors & Habits

Regular action patterns driven by other categories. These are core to identity and ego -- people define themselves by what they do.

Includes mental actions: "constantly overthinking", "comparing prices for hours", "reading every review".

**Category Checker:** Does this describe what the person DOES?

### 5. Demographics

Static facts: age, gender, location, income, education, family status. **LEAST important.** Often implied by the other four categories. Use last, for extra specificity only.

**Category Checker:** Does it fit a standard demographic category?

## What About Beliefs?

Beliefs are NOT a 6th category. Every belief decomposes into Experience + Emotion + Behavior.

When you spot a belief (e.g. "Natural products are always better"):

- **Experience:** "What happened?" -> Tried a chemical product that caused a reaction
- **Emotion:** "How does it make them feel?" -> Fearful, distrustful
- **Behavior:** "What do they do?" -> Only buys products labelled "natural", reads every ingredient list

## Core vs Sub Avatars

- **Core Avatar** -- Anchored by 1 category (usually a desire). Broad enough to represent a meaningful segment.
- **Sub-Avatar** -- Layers 2+ Core 5 categories onto a core avatar. More categories = more specific = more relatable = higher conversion at lower budget.

## Niche Gap Test

A sub-avatar only earns its place if it has a clear niche gap: a specific purchase situation, use case, or real-world context that makes its relationship with the product meaningfully different from the rest of the core avatar.

If two sub-avatars would buy in the same moment, for the same reason, and with the same objections, they are not two sub-avatars. They are one situation wearing different personality adjectives.

| Good Sub-Avatar (Situation-Based) | Bad Sub-Avatar (Trait-Based) |
|---|---|
| Airbnb host restocking cleaning supplies between guest changeovers | Eco-conscious |
| New parent childproofing the cupboard under the sink | Health-focused |
| Flat-sharer who has run out of cupboard space for bottles | Minimalist |
| Dog owner who mops the kitchen floor every day | Clean freak |

Ask these 3 questions before keeping or creating a sub-avatar:

1. Would this person's ad look fundamentally different from every other sub-avatar's ad?
2. Does this person face a unique objection no other sub-avatar faces?
3. Could I describe this sub-avatar by their situation alone, without personality adjectives?

If the answer is "no" to any of those questions, the niche gap is probably weak.

When multiple trait-based sub-avatars share the same purchase situation, merge them into a single situation-based sub-avatar and keep only the strongest hooks, objections, and value props inside that clearer profile.

## The Rule of One

One avatar, one desire, one message per ad. Never try to speak to multiple avatars in a single piece of creative.

## Avatar Scale

Spectrum from hyper-specific to universal:

- **"One Pepe"** (hyper-specific) -- Very narrow sub-avatar. Low budget needed to convert. Best for targeted campaigns.
- **"All Pepes"** (universal) -- Broad core avatar. Needs better product/offer/brand to convert at scale.

More specific = lower budget needed. More broad = better creative and offer needed.

## How Many Avatars

- **$0-100k/month:** 1 core avatar + sub-avatars
- **$100k-1M/month:** 1-3 core avatars
- **$1M+/month:** 3-10 core avatars

## Getting Avatars Right

"Get the avatars right, and the angles write themselves." Research deeply. Use review mining, competitor analysis, forums, social listening. The best avatars come from real customer language, not marketing imagination.
