# Execution

Was it the strategy or the execution that failed? Read this before retiring any angle. Based on the Evolve framework.

## The execution problem

You can have the concept, angle, variation, format, and testing method all right, understand the avatar, and the ad still flops. The execution problem is when the angle is correct but the WAY it was executed (the words and the content) was not good enough to convert.

The expensive failure: abandoning a good angle because it was executed badly. Before calling an angle a loser, rule out execution.

## Why ads fail on execution

1. **No emotional impact.** It does not make anyone feel anything. The closer it sounds to the conversation already in the avatar's head, the higher the impact.
2. **No curiosity.** Nothing gives people a reason to stop and click.
3. **No suspense** (video only). Nothing makes them keep watching.
4. **Too complex.** Above a 6th grade reading level.
5. **Empty claims.** "The best X" with no reason to believe it.
6. **Bossy claims.** Telling strangers what to do ("stop scrolling").
7. **Repetitive claims.** The same claim, said the same way competitors say it. See `marketing-frameworks.md` (market sophistication).
8. **Written, not spoken.** Copy that reads like an essay instead of how people talk. For voiceover this is fatal.
9. **Written for yourself.** Your words, not the customer's exact words.
10. **Angle too broad.** Not relatable enough; slid back into copying templates.
11. **Angle everyone else is saying.** A competitive angle with no copy skill to stand out.
12. **Wrong visual style for the audience.** What looks good to a 25 year old designer may not land with a 60 year old buyer. Align the lens with the customer.
13. **Bad format.** A format failing does not mean the angle failed. The simpler the format, the better the message and copy have to be.

## Strategy or execution? The diagnostic

**Test more than one format per angle.** Evolve's set: photo plus headline; static graphic design; voiceover with b-roll. Each format test is its own concept (see `angles-and-testing.md`).

- All formats fail: likely a **strategy** problem (the angle or the avatar).
- Some fail but one gives a direction: **execution**. Keep the angle and communicate it better.

Check the tracker before deciding: filter `testing/creative-tracker.csv` by `angle` and read the `format` column. An angle that has only ever run in one format has not been diagnosed yet.

**Validate execution first with proven structures.** Before testing new avatars or angles, use execution styles that already won: winning structures from your own account, and similar winning structures from the wider market (`copywriting.md` lists image ad structures). This removes some of the guessing about whether the angle works. Two cautions: a very different avatar may need a different structure; and study ads that are scaling further, not just the ones you are stuck on.

## How long to keep an angle

1. First Discovery batch did not work, but you are confident in the avatar: run a second batch on it with Deep Iteration (or a different format) to give it a real shot.
2. After the feedback loop, if you are confident the failure was execution: a third batch is allowed.
3. Beyond three, treat it as a sign you cannot execute this angle yet and move to another.

Winning angles can take as many batches as they keep earning, but watch for iteration overload: step away to other angles and come back fresh. Optimising existing ads after a break often beats making new ones.

## Soft benchmarks

Seen across winning ads. They are guides, not verdicts:

- **CTR at least 1%**
- Hook rate at least 25% (video only)
- Hold rate at least 30% of the hook rate (video only)

Below these, what you said or showed is probably not resonating enough for people to stop. Some ads break the rule both ways.

## Questions before a brief ships

Every question without a marker applies to statics as well as video.

- Does the first second or two hook the RIGHT person in?
- Is this an ad the potential customer would want to see?
- Does the copy create a clear image in the reader's mind?
- Does it feel native to the platform?
- Does it label the type of person it is for?
- Would it make someone smile or laugh?
- Is it relatable?
- Does it look like an ad? (It should not.)
- Gut check: does it feel authentic?
- Which learnings from previous winners did it apply?
- Does it educate the reader, creatively, on how to get their core desire?
- Has escalation been used to exaggerate the claim in a clear, relatable way?
- Does it tell a story the avatar cares about, entertain them enough to share, or educate them with a believable new mechanism?
- Does it separate you from everyone else in the category?
- Does the first 1 to 2 seconds KEEP them hooked? (video only)

## The brief

One brief per concept:

1. **Concept:** what this ad set tests
2. **Angle(s):** the selling strategy
3. **Testing method:** Discovery Batch, Deep Iteration, or A/B Split
4. **Format:** how it is delivered
5. **Brief:** per variation, the messaging (the words on the ad) and the content (the image or scene described)
6. **Sizes:** always 1080x1920 (9:16); image safe zone 1080x1080 (1:1); video safe zone 1080x1350 (4:5)
7. **Notes** for whoever renders it
8. **Examples:** reference ads and what to take from each
9. **Asset folder:** every asset the ad needs in one place
10. **Platform copy:** primary text 1 and 2, headline 1 and 2 (the 3:2:2 rule)
