# Marketing Frameworks

Each framework paired with its ideal awareness level:

| Framework | Best For | Awareness Level |
|---|---|---|
| Problem-Solution | Revealing the problem, product as solution | Problem Aware |
| Hero/Villain | Position villain (ingredient/habit/competitor), product as hero | Solution Aware |
| Us vs Them | In-group identity creation | Product Aware |
| Before/After | Show transformation (Meta compliance note: avoid misleading before/after imagery) | Problem -> Solution Aware |
| Social Proof | Volume of happy customers, UGC | Product -> Most Aware |
| Authority | Expert endorsement, credentials | Solution Aware |
| Mechanism | Unique "how" (ingredient, process, tech) | Solution Aware (Stage 3+ markets) |
| Identity | "This is for people who ___" | Problem Aware |
| Scarcity | Limited availability | Most Aware |
| Educational | Teach value, product as natural next step | Unaware -> Problem Aware |

## Three Strategic Responses for Sophisticated Markets (Stage 3-5)

### 1. New Mechanism
Reveal the "secret thing" behind your solution. Tech, process, or ingredient angle. Works when the market has heard every claim but hasn't seen your HOW.

- A new mechanism gives people **new hope** that the claim will finally be delivered. Sophisticated customers crave a clear understanding of HOW something works.
- Where to look: a new delivery method or format; an innovative approach to an existing problem; an uncommon component; a specific combination of existing elements that improves the result; a contrarian approach that challenges conventional wisdom.
- Shape: "Not another X, but the first one that..." Fernwood: not another eco spray, but the first pod that turns the bottle you already own into one that cleans like the old one.
- Mistakes: over technical explanation that alienates; claiming uniqueness for an industry standard process; explaining the mechanism without connecting it to what the customer gets. A portable music player did not sell on its storage spec; "1,000 songs in your pocket" sold it.
- Needs: a real, demonstrable difference and proof.

### 2. New Information
Educate to create problem awareness. Reframe the problem entirely. Works when the market thinks they understand the problem but doesn't.

- Shapes: the previously unknown "why" behind the problem; a reframe of the whole problem; the hidden obstacle others miss.
- Freshness: published or discovered in the last 12 to 24 months and not yet common in the market's messaging.
- For each finding record: the source with publication and date; why it is new (the sophistication gap); how to use it ethically (educational positioning); three hooks that educate and pique curiosity without fear mongering.
- Requires real expertise. Surface level education or overly promotional content destroys credibility.
- **Ethics rule:** double check your facts, and double check AI research. Never mislead to make information feel new.
- Needs: subject expertise and a long commitment to authority.

### 3. New Identity
Lifestyle movement, tribal belonging. Redefine what buying says about the customer. Works when every logical argument has been exhausted.

- Shapes: redefine what buying says about the person; "Not another X, but for people who..."; cultural, tribal, or anti-establishment angle.
- Fails when it feels inauthentic, when values do not match what the brand actually does, or when it tries to appeal to everyone. Detail in `belief-framework.md` (Identification Framework).
- Needs: brand strategy and authentic storytelling.

## Market Sophistication Stages (Schwartz)

**Sophistication** is the market's overall experience of, and exposure to, other solutions before you. How many similar products has it been told about? How many has it tried? When every option looks the same there is no obvious choice, so people pick at random. A well branded option or a genuinely new mechanism grabs attention and becomes the attractive choice.

### Four Rules

1. **Sophistication is not awareness.** Awareness is one person's knowledge of the problem and product (`../strategy/awareness-levels.md`); sophistication is what the whole market has already heard.
2. **You do not test sophistication.** It just exists. Research tells you the stage; ads respond to it.
3. **The avatar and sophistication gap.** A perfectly defined avatar, a clear desire, and a product that delivers can still fail if the message does not break through the market's sophistication (see below).
4. **Markets sophisticate faster than ever.** Feedback and word of mouth now scale as fast as selling does.

### How to Determine the Stage

1. Analyse the product and the problems it solves.
2. List everyone selling the same kind of product.
3. List everyone solving the same problem another way.
4. Collect their claims and ads side by side and look for the angle the market has not seen. Research is how you experience the sophistication from the customer's seat.

Record the stage and the chosen response in `../strategy/awareness-levels.md`.

### The Five Stages

- **Stage 1: First to Market** -- Simple claims work. "This product does X."
- **Stage 2: Competition Emerges** -- Amplify promises. "This does X BETTER."
- **Stage 3: Market Saturation** -- Need new mechanisms. "This does X through [unique process]."
- **Stage 4: Mechanism Competition** -- Enhance/elaborate mechanisms. "Our [process] is better because..."
- **Stage 5: Complete Sophistication** -- Only emotional connection and identity works. "For the kind of person who..."

An alternative mapping of the later stages: Stage 3 needs unique mechanisms, Stage 4 needs new discoveries or expanded benefits, Stage 5 needs complete repositioning or a new market. Both mappings agree that Stages 3 to 5 need one of the three responses above, not bigger claims.

**Most eCommerce markets are Stage 3-5.** Simple claims and amplified promises are already exhausted. This is why avatar work alone isn't enough -- even with perfect avatars and clear desires, you fail if you can't break through the sophistication level.

### The Avatar-Market Sophistication Gap

Avatars tell you WHO to talk to and WHAT they want. Market sophistication tells you HOW to say it. Both are required:

- **Great avatar + wrong sophistication response** = message that understands the customer but sounds like every other brand. Gets ignored.
- **Right sophistication response + wrong avatar** = clever creative that speaks to no one in particular. Gets attention but doesn't convert.
- **Great avatar + right sophistication response** = message that feels personally relevant AND cuts through market noise. This is what wins.

Use the Three Strategic Responses (above) matched to your market's sophistication stage. At Stage 3+, default to New Mechanism. At Stage 5, lean on New Identity.
