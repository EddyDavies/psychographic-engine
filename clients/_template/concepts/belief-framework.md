# Belief Framework

Three elements determine three parts of every ad:

| Element | Determines | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **Desire** | Ad **content** | What does the customer want? |
| **Identity** | Ad **visuals** | Who does the customer want to be? |
| **Beliefs** | Ad **development** | What must the customer believe to buy? |

## Why Beliefs Matter

If your customer does not have BELIEF in your product, your mechanism, or your claims -- they will NOT buy. Desire gets attention. Identity creates connection. But belief closes the sale.

## Belief-Building Methods

Use these in sequence to build conviction:

### 1. Yes Train

Start with statements the prospect already agrees with. Each "yes" makes the next agreement easier. Build momentum before introducing your claim.

### 2. Symptoms

Describe the prospect's situation so accurately they feel understood. When you name their symptoms precisely, they believe you understand the solution too.

### 3. Authority

Credentials, expert endorsements, certifications, years of experience. External proof that your claims are credible.

### 4. Logic

Step-by-step reasoning that makes the mechanism feel inevitable. "If A is true, and B is true, then C must follow."

## Belief Audit Questions

Use these when developing ads or landing pages:

- What are the current market beliefs about this problem?
- What does the prospect currently believe about products like ours?
- What are the most common objections?
- Which of our claims sound too good to be true?
- What would need to be true for a sceptic to buy?

## Identification Framework

Identity determines visuals. Use these questions to get the visual world right:

- **Role:** What role does your product offer to your prospect? (e.g. "the prepared parent", "the sophisticated host")
- **Instrument:** How does the product become an instrument for that role?
- **Environment:** Where does my ideal customer want to be? (This determines setting/backdrop)
- **Appearance:** What does my ideal customer want to look like? (This determines the model/talent)
- **Feeling:** What does my ideal customer want to feel? (This determines the story arc)
- **Expression:** What self-image do my customers want to portray? (This determines tone and aesthetic)

## Map of Consciousness Reference

Hawkins' Map of Consciousness provides a scale of emotional states from low energy (shame, guilt, fear) to high energy (courage, love, joy, peace).

In practice: ads often start at a lower emotional state (fear, frustration) to create recognition, then escalate to a higher state (courage, pride, joy) to motivate action. The landing page and ad must align on one desire, one avatar, one message -- and move the prospect up the emotional scale toward purchase.
