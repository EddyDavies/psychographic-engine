# Desire Forces Framework

You cannot CREATE desires. You can only CHANNEL existing desires onto your product.

All desires come from the interplay of permanent forces and forces of change. Understanding these forces lets you find desires that already exist, assess their power, and channel them toward your product.

## Permanent Forces

Two types of permanent forces drive human desire:

### 6 Mass Instincts

Hardwired human drives that never change (see also: avatars README).

**Power Ranking** (highest to lowest reach/urgency):

1. **Health** -- Survival, staying alive, avoiding disease
2. **Status** -- Being important, respected, admired
3. **Sex** -- Being attractive, finding/keeping a mate
4. **Comfort** -- Making life easier, avoiding pain, saving time
5. **Control** -- Having power, predicting outcomes, reducing uncertainty
6. **Belonging** -- Fitting in, being accepted, being part of a group

Higher-ranked instincts access larger audiences and trigger faster action. When possible, elevate your product positioning toward Health or Status.

### 6 Mass Technological Problems

Permanent frustrations created by technology that never fully resolve:

1. **Complexity** -- Too many options, features, or steps
2. **Overwhelm** -- Too much information, too many decisions
3. **Fragility** -- Things break, fail, or degrade
4. **Maintenance** -- Everything requires ongoing effort to keep working
5. **Incompatibility** -- Things don't work together as expected
6. **Obsolescence** -- What worked yesterday stops working tomorrow

These sit alongside the Mass Instincts as permanent sources of desire. Products that solve technological problems tap into frustrations that never go away.

## Forces of Change

Two forces that shift HOW permanent desires get fulfilled:

### Style Trends

Cultural and aesthetic shifts that change what "good" looks like. A desire for Status is permanent, but what signals status changes constantly (fashion, design, language, platforms).

### Mass Education

New information that changes what people believe works. A desire for Health is permanent, but what people believe is healthy shifts with new research, influencers, and cultural movements.

## Desire Channeling

Desires emerge from combinations of forces:

- **Mass Instinct + Style Trend** -- e.g. Status desire channeled through a trending aesthetic
- **Mass Tech Problem + Mass Education** -- e.g. Overwhelm frustration channeled by new awareness of simplification methods
- **Mass Instinct + Mass Education** -- e.g. Health desire channeled by new research on gut health

Your job: identify which forces are active for your market, then channel the resulting desire onto your product.

## Trending vs Permanent Desires

- **Permanent desires** come from Mass Instincts and Mass Tech Problems alone. They never disappear. Safer but more competitive.
- **Trending desires** emerge when Forces of Change interact with Permanent Forces. They have momentum but can fade.

### Headwinds & Tailwinds

- **Tailwinds** -- cultural, educational, or trend forces pushing in your product's direction. Align with as many as possible.
- **Headwinds** -- forces pushing against your positioning. Avoid fighting multiple headwinds simultaneously.

The best positioning aligns a permanent desire with multiple tailwinds and zero headwinds.

## Desire Calendar

Desires shift seasonally. Health peaks in January. Comfort peaks in winter. Sex/attractiveness peaks before summer. Status peaks around gift-giving holidays.

Map your product's core desires against the calendar to identify natural demand peaks and plan creative accordingly.

## Elevation Strategy

Every product can be positioned at any of the 6 instinct levels. The Elevation Map (see product template) captures where a product currently sits and where it could move.

### Moving UP the hierarchy

Repositioning toward higher-ranked instincts (e.g. Comfort -> Status -> Health) increases potential scale and emotional urgency. A cleaning spray positioned as Comfort ("a cleaner that's nice to use") has less reach than one positioned as Status ("the spray that looks good on the counter") or Health ("no harsh chemicals around your kids").

### Moving DOWN the hierarchy

Reduces emotional urgency and perceived value. Generally avoid unless the higher positioning isn't credible.

### The Authenticity Rule

The elevation must feel authentic. Force it, and customers sense the manipulation. Test elevated positioning with real customer language -- if customers already talk about your product at that instinct level, the elevation is credible. If you have to invent the connection, it probably won't land.
